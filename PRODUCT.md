# CasePilot

Case management web app for solo lawyers and small law firms. Cases, documents, deadlines, AI document analysis, team collaboration. The simple alternative to Clio, MyCase, PracticePanther.

Frontend only (Next.js 16, React 19, Tailwind 4, Zustand). Backend is built separately. Target launch April 2026.

## Users

**Primary: Solo lawyers and small-firm attorneys, 24-45.** Litigation, corporate, criminal defense, family, immigration, real estate. Mid-level tech savvy, comfortable with basic digital tools but not power users. Currently mixing paper files, spreadsheets, and folders. They want fewer admin hours, not more dashboards.

**Secondary: Legal assistants and paralegals.** Handle scheduling, document filing, case updates on behalf of attorneys. Often the heavier daily user of the tool.

**The job they hire CasePilot for:** find the right document or detail in seconds, never miss a court date, get a fast read on a long contract or filing, and keep the assistant looped in. The product wins by being faster than what they have now, not by having more features.

## Tone & voice

Plain, professional, exact. The voice of a senior paralegal — competent, calm, undecorated. Short sentences. Real nouns and verbs (filing, hearing, motion, discovery), not SaaS abstractions ("synergy," "empower," "supercharge"). Never marketing-AI ("unleash," "transform your practice"). Confident enough to leave whitespace.

## Anti-references

The product positioning is *anti* these aesthetic and product reflexes. Avoid them on every surface.

- **Clio / MyCase / PracticePanther** — bloated, busy, every feature competes for attention. CasePilot must look quieter and faster.
- **The legal-tech cobalt-on-white reflex.** Cobalt blue on flat corporate white is the lazy training-data answer for "legal software." Our chrome is a deeper editorial navy paired with warm cream and a single warm-amber accent — productized, not corporate.
- **The legal-tech navy-and-gold reflex.** "Trust" via gold leaf and serif logos = barristers' chambers cosplay. Our amber is a vivid action color, not a brushed-gold trust signal. Reject.
- **AI-pill / sparkle-icon decoration.** "✨ AI-powered" badges, chat-bubble flourish, gradient borders around the AI surface. AI here is a quiet utility on documents, not a feature flag in a star.
- **Identical feature-card grids.** Six cards, icon + heading + paragraph, each in a different rainbow tint (purple, green, amber, red, blue). Marketing-deck slop.
- **Glassmorphism + gradient hero.** Frosted nav, decorative purple-to-blue radial behind a centered headline. Generic SaaS landing.
- **Hero-metric template.** "12,000 hours saved · 4.9 stars · SOC 2." Pure cliché.

## Strategic principles

1. **Speed over surface.** A lawyer should find what they need before they finish noticing the UI. Density is fine when it serves search and recognition.
2. **Document-respect.** The work is the documents and the dates. The product chrome should never compete with them. Treat case titles like editorial headlines, not table cells.
3. **Calm, not corporate.** Warm cream workspace inside navy chrome — not glacial gray-blue end-to-end. The screen should feel like a serious tool wrapped around a well-lit desk, not a ticketing system.
4. **Functional color, not decorative color.** Color carries meaning (status, priority, alert). It does not get sprinkled to "make features pop."
5. **Trust through restraint.** Lawyers handle confidential work. Loud animation, gimmicks, and excitement-design erode trust. Quiet confidence builds it.
6. **Assistant-first.** Anywhere a flow can be operated by an assistant on behalf of a lawyer, design for the assistant's path first, then verify it doesn't slow down the lawyer.

## Out of scope

Mobile native, billing/invoicing, client portal, public marketing site beyond the landing page. Backend/API.
